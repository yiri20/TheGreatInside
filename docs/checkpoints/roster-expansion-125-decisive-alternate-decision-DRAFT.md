**Explicit finding on witness-independence, as instructed**: this
session confirms there is exactly **one** original ancient witness with
any plausible direct information about Chandragupta's court —
Megasthenes. Strabo, Diodorus, and Arrian are transmitters of that one
witness, filtered through their own selection and paraphrase, not
additional independent witnesses. Plutarch's Androcottus anecdote and
Justin's Sandrocottus anecdote are each internally distinct narrative
strands (different content, not obviously derived from Megasthenes), so
they are *not* simply more repetitions of the Strabo/Arrian material —
but each is, on its own, a single later transmission of an unidentified
and likely oral-tradition-shaped source, roughly 400-700 years after the
events it describes, and Justin's version is explicitly bundled with
supernatural/omen content that modern scholarship treats as
unreliable-genre material.

### 1.C Direct comparison with Ashoka

**The single most important finding of this pack, not fully weighed in
the prior session**: Ashoka left an extensive, genuinely contemporaneous
epigraphic record — more than 30 edicts on pillars, boulders, and cave
walls across his own empire, in his own reign, whatever their
self-presentational bias. **Chandragupta left virtually no securely
dated inscriptions from his own reign at all** (confirmed this session)
— the only material remains are a wooden-palace foundation and pottery/
coin finds, neither of which carries personal-conduct content. This
means Chandragupta's evidentiary problem is not simply "a different
kind of thin evidence" — on the contemporaneous/primary-source axis
specifically, **his own record is thinner than Ashoka's**, not richer.

His one advantage is Megasthenes: a genuine, non-self-interested,
near-contemporary foreign observer, which Ashoka's record entirely
lacks. But this session's direct check of the primary transmission found
that advantage delivers far less than the prior session assumed:
- The richest personality-relevant Megasthenes-derived material (fear of
  assassination, security customs) is **not explicitly candidate-named**
  in its primary source.
- The one explicitly candidate-named Megasthenes-derived fact (camp
  discipline) is narrow and administrative, not interpersonal.
- Two later, single-transmission anecdotes (Plutarch's Androcottus
  remark, Justin's Sandrocottus/Alexander episode) are candidate-named
  but each rests on one distant, orally-shaped source, and one is
  explicitly bundled with supernatural material this project's own
  precedent (the *Ashokavadana*) already commits to excluding.
- The personality-richest late-life material (Jain *sallekhana*) is not
  merely late legend like the *Ashokavadana* — it carries a live,
  unresolved dispute about which person it concerns, a strictly worse
  problem than anything in Ashoka's own file.
- The rise-to-power/Chanakya material — intuitively the most promising
  domain for real interpersonal/strategic behavioral evidence — turns
  out to be the *weakest* attested, resting on a Sanskrit drama written
  600-1,000+ years later.

**Explicit answer to the required question**: *Does Chandragupta provide
enough candidate-specific behavioral evidence, with sufficient
provenance constraint, to make Protocol v1 scoring more defensible than
Ashoka?* **No.** The evidence structure does not materially improve
personality differentiation over Ashoka's own record — it trades
Ashoka's specific, acknowledged problem (rich contemporaneous
self-presentation, no independent observer of ordinary conduct) for a
different and, on the contemporaneous-primary-source axis, *thinner*
problem (almost no contemporaneous record of any kind in his own voice
or institutional context; one real but narrow external-observer fact;
a small number of single-transmission, centuries-later anecdotes, one of
which is explicitly entangled with excluded-category legendary material;
and a late-life tradition with a genuine identity dispute on top of its
lateness).

### 1.D Disposition

**`NO_PROVEN_REPLACEMENT`.**

Most behavior still rests on a single original witness whose surviving
fragments are largely generic-in-context rather than candidate-named;
the record's most personality-rich material is either later legend (with
one instance carrying a genuine, unresolved person-identity dispute) or
a single oral-tradition-shaped anecdote per data point; and — the
decisive new finding of this pack — Chandragupta's own contemporaneous
epigraphic/institutional record is thinner than Ashoka's, not richer.
The alternate does not materially improve on the primary.

## Part 2 — Rabban Bar Sauma decisive pack

### 2.A Provenance/transmission analysis

**Title/manuscript/textual tradition**: *The History of Mar Yahballaha
and Rabban Sauma* (Syriac), rediscovered only in the late 19th century
(confirmed prior session); a modern critical edition exists (Borbone,
translated by Parodi). **Composition date**: completed **1317-1319**
(confirmed prior session — shortly after Patriarch Yahballaha III's own
1317 death), roughly a generation (~23-25 years) after Bar Sauma's own
1294 death. **Authorship status**: anonymous East Syriac writer, member
of the same Church of the East community both subjects belonged to.

**Confirmed this session, correcting the prior session's framing**: the
work is not evenly "third-party witnessed" across its whole span. Per
its own modern editorial assessment (checked prior session, re-examined
here): the author "must have been an eyewitness of much of what he
relates" for the **earlier, monastic-community period** — meaning
material about Bar Sauma's vocation, his relationship with Markos, and
the life of their shared religious community plausibly draws on genuine
communal memory the author had direct access to. For the **European
mission specifically** — the single richest source of externally-dated,
diplomatically-confirmed material — the author explicitly "was able to
make use of Sauma's diary and indeed reproduces it verbatim at times."
**This means the European-journey material, including its most
behaviorally vivid scenes, is fundamentally self-reported by Bar Sauma
himself, embedded in a text written by someone else** — a materially
different claim than "independently witnessed."

**New finding this session, not established previously**: Rossabi's own
modern scholarly biography (*Voyager from Xanadu*, the standard English-
language treatment) explicitly states that Sauma's underlying notes are
"fragmentary" and "much edited by their original translator," and that
"readers of this book will rarely hear Sauma's own words" — meaning even
the surviving primary material is thinner and more heavily mediated than
a naive reading of the 1317-1319 History suggests, and Rossabi's own
narrative substantially reconstructs "what the traveler probably saw and
experienced" rather than quoting a stable primary text throughout.

**Are the specific dialogue scenes verbatim, reconstructed, or unknown?**
Checked directly against two of the richest candidate scenes:
- **The Rome cardinals' theological interrogation (1287)**: a detailed,
  multi-turn exchange (the cardinals' specific questions about apostolic
  succession in the East, Bar Sauma's answer naming Sts. Thomas, Thaddeus,
  and Mari, his cautious handling of Trinity/Christology questions, his
  decision to "cut them short" and redirect to his diplomatic purpose
  rather than be drawn into a heresy debate). **The anonymous Syriac
  author was not present in Rome** — this scene, however richly narrated,
  can only derive from Bar Sauma's own account relayed to his community
  (or his diary), i.e. it is **self-report**, not independent witness,
  despite appearing inside a text someone else compiled.
- **The Bordeaux Eucharist before Edward I**: similarly detailed (Edward
  kneeling, the specific liturgical actions, the banquet). **Same
  problem**: the Syriac author was not in Bordeaux. This is self-report.
  One possible independent echo was checked this session — a claim that
  Pope Nicholas IV later remarked that Bar Sauma's Mass "resembled those
  celebrated in the West, apart from the language" — but this session
  could not confirm that remark traces to an actual independent Vatican
  document rather than being itself relayed through the same Syriac
  History (i.e., Bar Sauma reporting what the Pope reportedly said to
  him). **Treated conservatively as unconfirmed-independent, not counted
  as an external behavioral witness.**

**Genre and institutional incentives**: composed within and for the
Church of the East community, likely serving to honor both men's
legacies (Yahballaha III as Patriarch, Bar Sauma as his celebrated
predecessor/envoy) — a real, if different, self-presentation incentive
from Ibn Battuta's individual dictated travelogue, but a incentive
nonetheless, and one that would plausibly favor flattering, faith-
affirming portrayals of both men's conduct (e.g., Bar Sauma's dignified
handling of the cardinals' theological trap) over less flattering detail.

**Corrected characterization, replacing the prior session's phrase**:
this is **not** accurately described as "a third-party-witnessed
biography" in the sense of independent behavioral observation. It is
more precisely: *a posthumous communal biography, drawing on genuine
institutional memory for the earlier/monastic period, and on Bar Sauma's
own self-reported diary/testimony — embedded in someone else's narration
— for the diplomatically richest European material.* The production
process is genuinely different from Ibn Battuta's solo-dictated Rihla,
but the resulting evidentiary shape for the *specific episodes with the
most personality-relevant content* is closer to self-report than the
prior session's framing acknowledged.
