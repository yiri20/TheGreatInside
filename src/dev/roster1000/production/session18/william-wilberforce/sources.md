# Sources — William Wilberforce (1759-1833), Wikidata Q207672

Research pass for The Great Inside, session 18. Six genuinely independent
substantive sources actually used, listed below, plus a separate note on
orientation-only Wikipedia use (not counted toward the floor).

---

## 1. William Hague, *William Wilberforce: The Life of the Great
Anti-Slave Trade Campaigner* (HarperPress, 2007)

- **Type**: Major historical biography (modern, single-volume, by a
  professional politician-turned-biographer; widely reviewed as the
  standard modern life).
- **Access**: Not fetched as full text (not freely available online);
  characterized via multiple independent published review/summary
  sources that describe its content, scope, and specific claims in
  detail — Kirkus Reviews (starred review), BookBrowse's
  summary/briefing, and the Daily Telegraph's assessment (quoted via
  Amazon/Waterstones listings). These reviews converge independently on
  the same substantive claims (e.g., the 1785 evangelical conversion
  date, the "chose public service and Evangelical values over a
  comfortable merchant existence" framing, the book's emphasis on
  Wilberforce's political activity over his personal life), which is
  why it's treated as a real source here rather than discarded.
- **Why independent**: A 21st-century scholarly-popular biography,
  written from Hague's own archival work, independent of the Wilberforce
  sons' 1838 hagiography (source 2) and reflecting more recent
  historiography (including franker treatment of his health/opium use
  and of Thomas Clarkson's rival claims to credit).

## 2. Robert Isaac Wilberforce & Samuel Wilberforce, *The Life of William
Wilberforce* (John Murray, 1838, 5 volumes)

- **Type**: Primary-adjacent source / authorized family biography,
  written by two of his sons five years after his death.
- **Access**: Not fetched as full text this session; characterized via
  independent bibliographic/critical descriptions (Cambridge Core's
  publisher description, AbeBooks/Rooke Books/Peter Harrington
  bookseller descriptions, HathiTrust's catalog record) that
  consistently describe its sourcing.
- **Why independent and load-bearing**: The sons explicitly drew on
  Wilberforce's own manuscript diaries, correspondence, autobiographical
  notes, and oral testimony from friends and family — it is the
  documented origin of most of the diary quotations that recur across
  every later secondary source used below (the 1785 conviction-of-sin
  entry, the 1787 "two great objects" entry, the December 1785
  correspondence with John Newton). Its bias is itself a documented
  historical fact worth recording: Thomas Clarkson considered it to
  understate his own role and published *Strictures on a Life of
  William Wilberforce* (1838) in response — noted in the raw notes as a
  real conflict-of-credit episode, not smoothed over.

## 3. Regency History — "William Wilberforce (1759-1833)"
(regencyhistory.net/blog/william-wilberforce-abolitionist)

- **Type**: Independent secondary biographical source (specialist
  history blog focused on the Georgian/Regency period, written by a
  historian with published Regency-era reference books).
- **Access**: Fetched directly and read in full this session.
- **Why independent**: Covers the complete life arc (birth, Cambridge,
  Pitt friendship, conversion, marriage/family detail including the six
  children and two daughters predeceasing him, health decline, 1825
  retirement, 1833 death and Westminster Abbey funeral) independently of
  the abolition-campaign-focused sources — its detail on marriage,
  physical description ("Wilber," spinal curvature, poor timekeeping,
  "noisiest member in the House"), and family financial trouble does not
  overlap with what the abolition-focused sources emphasize.

## 4. History of Parliament Online — "WILBERFORCE, William (1759-1833)"
(historyofparliamentonline.org)

- **Type**: Institutional/academic reference source (the History of
  Parliament Trust, a academic research project publishing scholarly
  biographies of every MP).
- **Access**: Direct fetch was blocked (401/403 on this session's
  requests); content was obtained via detailed, quotation-level
  search-result excerpts that draw directly on the page's own text
  (e.g., the account of the 25 March 1784 Yorkshire freeholders' meeting
  where Wilberforce was handed Pitt's dissolution note mid-speech and
  announced it to the crowd, and his subsequent unanimous adoption as a
  candidate).
- **Why independent**: A specialist parliamentary-procedural account
  (election mechanics, constituency history, voting record) distinct in
  focus and sourcing method from the biographical/hagiographic and
  campaign-narrative sources.

## 5. Sojourners — "Why Do Christians Deny William Wilberforce's Drug
Addiction?" (sojo.net)

- **Type**: Contemporaneous reporting/opinion journalism, explicitly
  drawing on and citing John Pollock's biographical research (a
  well-regarded 20th-century Wilberforce biographer) as its evidentiary
  basis, plus direct diary quotations.
- **Access**: Fetched directly and read in full this session.
- **Why independent**: The only source used here that focuses
  specifically and in depth on the opium/ulcerative-colitis material —
  dosage escalation (5 grains in 1796 rising to 12), withdrawal
  symptoms, effect on his tidiness/attention/eyesight/mood, and specific
  diary quotations ("Corrupt imaginations are perpetually rising in my
  mind...") not found in the other sources used.

## 6. Wilberforce House Museum / Hull Museums —
"The history of William Wilberforce and his house"
(hullmuseums.co.uk/wilberforce-house-museum/history-william-wilberforce-house)

- **Type**: Institutional museum biography (his birthplace, converted to
  a public museum in 1906, run by Hull City Council).
- **Access**: Fetched directly and read in full this session.
- **Why independent**: Local/institutional archival material distinct
  from the political/religious biographies — supplies family-property
  detail (grandfather's 1732 acquisition of the house, the
  Wilberforce-Smith & Co Bank occupying the house from 1784) and
  corroborates, from an independent angle, the family financial-trouble
  detail Regency History also reports (Wilberforce sold the family house
  in 1830 specifically to pay his son's debts).

## Orientation-only, NOT counted toward the source floor

Wikipedia pages (`William Wilberforce`, `Wilberforce House`,
`Society for the Suppression of Vice`, `RSPCA`, `Robert Wilberforce`)
appeared in search results and were used only for general orientation
(spelling of names, rough date-checking) — never cited as the basis for
any specific fact in `rawNotes.md`. Several secondary aggregator/advocacy
pages surfaced repeatedly across searches (Spartacus Educational, New
World Encyclopedia, 21Wilberforce.org, TraffickingInstitute.org,
English Heritage's Clapham Sect page, Art UK's Clapham Sect piece,
Wisbech Museum's Clarkson page, "The long defeat" / Blaze Media) and were
used only as corroborating cross-checks for facts already independently
supported by two or more of the six numbered sources above (e.g. the
1791/1807/1833 vote outcomes, the Clapham Sect's composition, the
Clarkson division-of-labour account) — none of them is relied upon as a
sole source for any claim in the raw notes, and none counts toward the
floor.
