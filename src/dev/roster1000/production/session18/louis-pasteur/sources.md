# Sources — Louis Pasteur (1822–1895), Wikidata Q529

Research conducted via live WebSearch/WebFetch, August 2026. Nine substantive
sources used (five fetched and read in full via WebFetch; four scholarly/
archival sources engaged through multiple independent, corroborating
WebSearch results that directly quote or closely paraphrase the underlying
text — noted per-entry below, per instruction to be honest about method).
Wikipedia was also used and is listed separately at the bottom as
orientation-only — it does NOT count toward the source floor.

---

### 1. Science History Institute — "Louis Pasteur" (Scientific Biographies series)
- **Type:** Institutional/scholarly biography
- **URL:** https://www.sciencehistory.org/education/scientific-biographies/louis-pasteur/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** Produced by the Science History Institute (a US
  research/museum organization dedicated to the history of science) as a
  standalone biographical treatment, not derivative of any single other
  source consulted here. Covers early life, crystallography, fermentation,
  the 1868 stroke, and vaccine work with its own framing and emphasis
  (e.g., specifically flags his "great experimenter" identity vs.
  theoretician, and his 1868 stroke/paralysis continuing to work).

### 2. Institut Pasteur (official) — "History"
- **Type:** Institutional biography / archival history
- **URL:** https://www.pasteur.fr/en/institut-pasteur/history
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** The institution Pasteur himself founded, publishing
  its own historical account and primary quotations (e.g., his "invisible
  dragon" description of rabies, his direct quote about not asking a
  sufferer's nationality). Distinct institutional voice/archive from the
  academic and journalistic sources below.

### 3. Institut Pasteur Research Journal — "The history of the first rabies vaccination in 1885"
- **Type:** Institutional archival account
- **URL:** https://www.pasteur.fr/en/research-journal/news/history-first-rabies-vaccination-1885
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** A dedicated, narrower institutional piece specifically
  on the Joseph Meister decision — distinct focus and additional detail
  (e.g., Émile Roux's initial refusal to participate, Jacques-Joseph
  Grancher's decisive persuasion) beyond the general institutional history
  page (#2).

### 4. Gavi, The Vaccine Alliance — "Scrutiny or public spectacle? How Louis Pasteur put the anthrax vaccine on trial"
- **Type:** Contemporaneous-style historical/science journalism, grounded in archival research
- **URL:** https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/scrutiny-or-public-spectacle-how-louis-pasteur-put-anthrax-vaccine-trial
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** An international vaccine-policy organization's own
  historical account of the Pouilly-le-Fort public trial (May–June 1881),
  written independently of the Institut Pasteur's own materials and
  explicitly engaging with the notebook-based revelation (published in the
  1970s) that Pasteur used a chemically inactivated vaccine developed by
  rival Toussaint/refined by his own assistant Chamberland, while publicly
  implying it was his own live-attenuated method.

### 5. History of Vaccines (The College of Physicians of Philadelphia) — "The Other Side of Louis Pasteur's Discoveries in Science and Medicine"
- **Type:** Institutional historical/critical analysis
- **URL:** https://historyofvaccines.org/blog/the-other-side-of-louis-pasteurs-discoveries-in-science-and-medicine/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** A medical-history museum's own critical treatment,
  distinct from Gavi's (#4) — enumerates specific documented human rabies
  trials beyond Meister (an unnamed adult man who abandoned treatment, a
  young girl who died after one injection), and directly engages with
  Geison's finding that Pasteur's public claim of extensive prior canine
  testing did not match his own notebooks.

### 6. Gerald L. Geison — *The Private Science of Louis Pasteur* (Princeton University Press, 1995)
- **Type:** Major scholarly biography, built from primary archival material
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (behind institutional
  paywalls at Project MUSE / De Gruyter / Chronicle of Higher Education —
  the Chronicle review returned HTTP 403). Engaged through multiple,
  independent WebSearch result sets (Princeton UP's own book description,
  Project MUSE listing, Chemical & Engineering News coverage of the
  notebooks, an SFGate obituary of Geison, and corroborating citations in
  sources #4 and #5 above) that consistently quote and describe the same
  underlying archival findings.
- **Why independent/substantive:** This is the foundational scholarly work
  behind nearly all "revisionist" material used in this research — it
  reconstructs 40+ years / 100+ volumes of Pasteur's private laboratory
  notebooks (held by his family and inaccessible to scholars until 1964,
  fully released only in the 1970s) and is the direct source for the
  anthrax-vaccine substitution episode, the rabies pre-clinical-testing
  discrepancy, and his instruction that his notebooks never be shown to
  anyone.

### 7. Patrice Debré — *Louis Pasteur* (Johns Hopkins University Press, English trans. 1998/2000)
- **Type:** Major full-length scholarly biography
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a monograph, not a web
  page). Engaged through multiple independent WebSearch results (publisher/
  bookseller descriptions from Amazon, Goodreads, AbeBooks, and a Times
  Higher Education review) that consistently describe its content and
  approach.
- **Why independent:** Written by a French physician/immunologist, drawing
  extensively and directly on Pasteur's own scientific notebooks and
  writings, offering — per its reviewers — a more balanced, less hagiographic
  full-life account than earlier 19th/early-20th-century biographies (e.g.,
  Pasteur's son-in-law René Vallery-Radot's). Used here mainly as
  corroboration for the overall life arc and for confirming that the
  "revisionist" material (Geison) sits within a broader, credentialed
  biographical consensus rather than being an outlier.

### 8. Académie des Sciences (Comptes Rendus Chimie) — "Louis Pasteur face à la maladie du ver à soie (1865–1870): du chimiste au biologiste"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed French scholarly journal article
- **URL (search-located):** https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/chimie/articles/10.5802/crchim.204/
- **Method:** Engaged via WebSearch synthesis of the abstract/summary
  content (not a full WebFetch of the PDF).
- **Why independent:** A specialist peer-reviewed history-of-science article
  focused narrowly on the five-year silkworm-disease (pébrine/flacherie)
  period — richer and more specific than the general biographies on this
  episode, including framing it as an "epistemological rupture" that
  converted Pasteur from a chemist into a biologist.

### 9. *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences* (Springer, peer-reviewed) — "Revisiting the Pouchet–Pasteur controversy over spontaneous generation: understanding experimental method"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed scholarly journal article
- **URL (search-located):** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40656-018-0229-7
- **Method:** Engaged via WebSearch synthesis of the article's content
  (not a full WebFetch of the paywalled article), cross-checked against a
  second independent academic treatment (Encyclopedia.com's "Historic
  Dispute" entry, itself drawing on the scholarly record) for the same
  1859–64 controversy.
- **Why independent:** A specialist 2018 methodological reassessment of the
  Pasteur–Pouchet dispute, distinct from the general biographical sources —
  supplies detail on the Academy commission's composition (friends/
  defenders of Pasteur), Pasteur's April 1864 Sorbonne lecture rhetoric,
  and the shared false assumption (both men believed boiling killed all
  microorganisms) that neither Pasteur nor Pouchet held correctly.
