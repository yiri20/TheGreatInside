# Sources — Louis Armstrong (1901–1971)

Research conducted via live web search/fetch, session 18. All sources below
were actually fetched and read (not guessed from snippets), except where a
search-results synthesis is explicitly noted as such.

## 1. Louis Armstrong House Museum — official Biography page
- **Type**: institutional biography (museum/archive-run)
- **URL**: https://www.louisarmstronghouse.org/biography/
- **Fetched directly.** Full chronological life narrative maintained by the
  Louis Armstrong House Museum at Queens College — the institution that holds
  Armstrong's own 60,000+ item personal archive (letters, tapes, scrapbooks,
  scores). Independent of any single biographer's interpretive frame; treated
  here as the closest thing to an institutional factual backbone (dates,
  named people, sequence of career moves).

## 2. Louis Armstrong House Museum — "I'm Still Louis Armstrong–Colored: Louis
   Armstrong and the Civil Rights Era" (virtual exhibit)
- **Type**: institutional biography / archival exhibit, drawing on primary
  documents (tapes, letters, contemporaneous press)
- **URL**: https://www.louisarmstronghouse.org/virtual-exhibits/im-still-louis-armstrong-colored-louis-armstrong-and-the-civil-rights-era/
- **Fetched directly.** A dedicated, source-cited curatorial essay (radio
  interview transcripts, Pittsburgh Courier coverage, Ebony profiles, taped
  conversations) specifically on Armstrong's civil-rights engagement —
  independent in scope and evidentiary base from the general biography above;
  goes well beyond the 1957 headline episode into the 1949 Zulu King
  controversy, the "Sleepy Time Down South" boycott, the Josephine Baker
  disagreement, and his post-1968 recalibration. This is the single richest
  primary-adjacent source used.

## 3. Terry Teachout, *Pops: A Life of Louis Armstrong* (2009) — via PBS
   NewsHour interview with the author and secondary review coverage
- **Type**: major historical/critical biography (secondary — accessed via an
  author interview transcript and review summaries, not the full book text)
- **URLs used**:
  - https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/conversation-terry-teachout-author-of-pops-a-life-of-louis-armstrong (fetched directly)
  - Goodreads/Kirkus/Jake Seliger review-summary material (search synthesis)
- Teachout's biography is the standard modern critical life, drawing on the
  Queens College archive and interviews; used here specifically for
  characterization detail (temper vs. public persona, "major-key artist,"
  handling of criticism, his self-written autobiography's authenticity) that
  is independent of the Armstrong House Museum's own institutional framing —
  a working biographer's outside interpretive judgment, not archive
  self-description.

## 4. The Nation — "Louis Armstrong Gets the Last Word on Louis Armstrong"
   (archive-focused profile/review, tied to the opening of the Louis
   Armstrong Center)
- **Type**: major historical-journalism profile drawing directly on primary
  archival material (tapes, scrapbooks, scores, annotated charts)
- **URL**: https://www.thenation.com/article/culture/louis-armstrong-archive/
- **Fetched directly.** Independent of Teachout and independent of the House
  Museum's own curated exhibits — a journalist's own engagement with the
  physical archive (scrapbooks from 1926 on, ~200 hand-annotated mixtapes,
  annotated performance scores, the Jelly Roll Morton scat-singing dispute,
  his personal copy of LeRoi Jones's *Blues People*). Provides evidence of
  Armstrong's own self-documentation practices and intellectual engagement
  not found in the other sources.

## 5. Jerry Jazz Musician — "Louis Armstrong and 'Gage'" (essay on Armstrong's
   lifelong marijuana use, drawing on his own letters/writings and a 1953
   interview with Bill Russell)
- **Type**: essay drawing on primary source material (Armstrong's own Library
  of Congress questionnaire responses, a recorded 1953 interview, trial
  transcripts)
- **URL**: https://www.jerryjazzmusician.com/louis-armstrong-gage/
- **Fetched directly.** Distinct subject matter (substance use, self-care
  philosophy, the 1930/1931 arrest and trial) not substantially covered by
  the other four sources; quotes Armstrong's own words directly ("Where other
  cats make their main ambition money or fame, I've made mine health").

## 6. Multi-source web-search synthesis: King Oliver/Chicago relationship,
   Joe Glaser management relationship, Karnofsky family/Star of David memoir,
   bebop-era conflict with Dizzy Gillespie, 1959 Italy heart attack
- **Type**: aggregated search-result synthesis across several independent
  outlets (64 Parishes encyclopedia entry on Joe "King" Oliver; Louis
  Armstrong House Museum's own West End Blog post "Joe Oliver the King";
  Jewish Currents / National Library of Israel blog / 64 Parishes "Satchmo
  and the Jewish Family" on the Karnofsky memoir; The Vintage News and other
  outlets on the Joe Glaser relationship; general biographical sources on the
  1959 Spoleto heart attack)
- **Not independently fetched page-by-page for this entry** — used as search
  synthesis rather than single-page fetches, so treated as corroborating/
  supplementary rather than one of the primary counted sources, but material
  drawn from it (all cross-corroborated across multiple named outlets in the
  results) is used in the raw notes below with that caveat noted inline.
