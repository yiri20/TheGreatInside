# Sources — Fyodor Dostoevsky (Q991)

Research conducted via live web search and fetch, 2026-08. All sources below
were actually retrieved (search-result synthesis or direct page fetch) this
session, not recalled from memory. Short ids in brackets are used for
citation in `rawNotes.md`.

## Counted independent substantive sources (6)

### 1. [FRANK] Joseph Frank's five-volume definitive scholarly biography of Dostoevsky (Princeton University Press, 1976–2002)
- **Type**: scholarly biography (accessed via reviews/summaries/publisher
  descriptions, not the full text directly).
- **What was fetched**: WebSearch aggregating results from Princeton
  University Press's own book pages (press.princeton.edu, publisher
  descriptions of *The Years of Ordeal 1850–1859* and *The Stir of
  Liberation 1860–1865*), a Washington Post review ("Dostoevsky: Convict and
  Convert," 1984), a second Washington Post review ("Back From the House of
  the Dead," 1986), and a Village Voice review ("Feodor's Guide: Joseph
  Frank's Dostoevsky").
- **Independence note**: Frank's biography is widely regarded as the
  definitive scholarly life of Dostoevsky, built from primary Russian
  archival material, correspondence, and contemporary testimony over
  decades of research — a different evidentiary base from the derivative
  encyclopedia summaries below, and it is the specific source that
  supplied the volume-level chronology (arrest → Peter-and-Paul Fortress →
  trial → mock execution → Siberian hard labor → army service → first
  marriage) used to structure the Siberian-exile period in `rawNotes.md`.

### 2. [ANNA-DIARY] Anna Grigoryevna Dostoevskaya, shorthand diary of 1867 (published 1923) and *Reminiscences* (published 1925)
- **Type**: primary autobiographical/diary material, written by his wife
  who lived with him daily for the last 14 years of his life.
- **What was fetched**: direct page fetch of a book-review/summary blog
  (thediaryjunction.blogspot.com, "The Diary Review: Quarrelling with
  Fyodor," 2018) that quotes and paraphrases specific diary entries
  (dated May 2, 1867, among others), plus WebSearch results describing the
  diary's origin, scope, and the couple's 1867 flight abroad.
- **Independence note**: this is Dostoevsky's own household's day-to-day
  eyewitness record, entirely distinct from any external biographer's
  account — the only source in this set written contemporaneously, in the
  moment, by someone living in the same room as him.

### 3. Encyclopedia.com, "Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky" (Encyclopedia of World Biography, reference-work entry)
- **URL**: https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/russian-and-eastern-european-literature-biographies/fyodor-mikhailovich-dostoevsky
- **Type**: major reference/historical biography (long-form, citation-dense
  encyclopedic biography, not a stub).
- **Fetched directly** (full page content retrieved and read). Supplied the
  single most complete chronological backbone used in this research — birth,
  military academy, father's death, Petrashevsky Circle, exile, both
  marriages, journal editing, the Suslova affair, gambling, all major
  novels with dates, the ideological shift, and death.
- **Independence note**: an independently authored, separately edited
  reference biography (Gale/Encyclopedia of World Biography), not a mirror
  of Frank's book or of Wikipedia's phrasing — its own synthesis of primary
  sources with a different structure and emphasis (notably stronger on
  intellectual/philosophical content, e.g. the Freud theory of his epilepsy,
  the "polyphonic" narrative-method discussion).

### 4. [YALE-BIO] Yale University course page biography of Dostoevsky
- **URL**: https://meek.yalepages.org/dostoevsky_bio.html
- **Type**: institutional/academic biography (university course-support
  page).
- **Fetched directly** (full page content retrieved and read).
- **Independence note**: an independently written academic synthesis, used
  here mainly as a cross-check against Encyclopedia.com and Frank-derived
  facts (both agreed closely on dates and sequence, which increases
  confidence in the shared chronology) and as the source for a few details
  emphasized differently here — e.g. his mother's religious pilgrimages
  with him as a child, and the description of his father as "tyrannical and
  domineering."

### 5. [UBC-WIKI] "The Mock-execution of Fyodor Dostoevsky," University of British Columbia Wiki
- **URL**: https://wiki.ubc.ca/The_Mock-execution_of_Fyodor_Dostoevsky
- **Type**: institutional (university) historical wiki article, itself
  citing primary letters and Frank's biography.
- **Fetched directly.**
- **Independence note**: focused narrowly and in more procedural detail on
  the December 22, 1849 mock-execution sequence (order of the three groups,
  who stood with Dostoevsky, the sequence of the sword-breaking ritual)
  than the general biographies above; supplemented by a second, independent
  fetch of Lit Hub's essay on the same event (litHub.com, "On the
  Terrifying Hoax Execution That Haunted Dostoevsky's Writing"), which
  supplied the direct quote from Dostoevsky's letter to his brother Mikhail
  written hours after the reprieve.

### 6. [RUSSIANLIFE] "Dostoyevsky's Brilliant Wife Anna," Russian Life magazine
- **URL**: https://russianlife.com/the-russia-file/dostoyevskys-brilliant-wife-anna/
- **Type**: contemporaneous long-form historical/biographical magazine
  journalism, drawing on Anna's own memoirs and later scholarship on her
  publishing career.
- **Fetched directly.**
- **Independence note**: distinct focus from source #2 (Anna's own diary
  text) — this piece covers Anna's later career as Dostoevsky's publisher
  and business manager (the first solo female-owned publishing house in
  Russia) and includes direct quotes from Dostoevsky's later letters to her
  (e.g. his 1876 acknowledgment "everything is in your power alone... I so
  highly value and trust in your mind"), material not found in the diary
  excerpts alone.

## Additional supporting material (search-synthesis only, not fetched as standalone pages, used to corroborate/extend the 6 sources above — not separately counted toward the source floor)

- WebSearch aggregation of Fyodor Stellovsky publishing-contract details
  (the 1866 "write a novel in one month or lose all publishing rights for
  nine years" deadline that produced *The Gambler*), drawing on
  26reads.com and other secondary retellings — corroborated independently
  by [ANNA-DIARY]-adjacent sources and [FRANK]'s own volume descriptions.
- WebSearch aggregation on the Belinsky/*Poor Folk* episode and the
  subsequent falling-out over *The Double* (New World Encyclopedia,
  Britannica snippet, The London Magazine essay on *Poor Folk*).
- WebSearch aggregation on the Dostoevsky–Turgenev feud (Russia Beyond
  "Literary wars" article; fyodor-dostoevsky.com), including the direct
  quotes "a sour smell and hospital stench" / "psychological nitpicking"
  attributed to Turgenev, and Dostoevsky's own "buy a telescope" jab.
- WebSearch aggregation on his father Mikhail Andreevich Dostoevsky's
  contested 1839 death (murder-by-serfs vs. stroke/heart attack), drawing
  on multiple secondary retellings citing brother Andrey Dostoevsky's own
  account.
- WebSearch aggregation on his 1876–1880 solo periodical *A Writer's Diary*
  (Wikipedia, Northwestern University Press book description, a blog
  essay "The World's First Blog Before the Internet").
- WebSearch aggregation on the June 1880 Pushkin Monument speech and its
  reception (Wikipedia's "Dostoevsky's Pushkin Speech" article; a
  history blog "facesofhistory.com").
- WebSearch aggregation on his gambling-era letters to Anna from
  Wiesbaden/Baden-Baden (theamericanreader.com's posting of the actual
  16 April 1871 letter; classicliterature.substack.com's retelling), used
  to corroborate and extend [ANNA-DIARY]'s account of the gambling years
  and its documented end-date (April 24, 1871).
- WebSearch aggregation on his brother Mikhail's 1864 death and the
  ~20,000-ruble debt left to Dostoevsky to cover, plus his ongoing support
  of his stepson Pasha and brother Nikolai (drawing on OrthodoxWiki, New
  World Encyclopedia, and a Bloggers Karamazov essay on the 1867
  honeymoon).
