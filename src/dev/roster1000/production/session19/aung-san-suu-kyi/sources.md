# Sources — Aung San Suu Kyi (b. 1945), Wikidata Q36740

Research conducted via live WebSearch/WebFetch, August 2026. Aung San Suu Kyi
is a LIVING person; per this project's safety rule, only tendencies/actions
that published accounts describe directly are recorded (never inferred
health, private relationships, or psychological conditions). She is also a
morally complex, internationally contested figure — this research documents
BOTH her decades of well-documented pro-democracy sacrifice AND the
widely-criticized conduct of her later political career (the Rohingya
crisis response, the ICJ appearance), factually and evidence-based, with
neither side sanitized or omitted, matching this project's existing
discipline for figures like Genghis Khan.

Nine substantive sources used (five fetched and read in full via WebFetch;
four primary-source/biographical works engaged through multiple independent,
corroborating WebSearch result sets that directly quote or closely
paraphrase the underlying text — noted per-entry below, per instruction to
be honest about method). Wikipedia was also used and is listed separately at
the bottom as orientation-only — it does NOT count toward the source floor.

---

### 1. Human Rights Watch — "Burma: Chronology of Aung San Suu Kyi's Detention"
- **Type:** International human-rights organization, institutional chronology
- **URL:** https://www.hrw.org/news/2010/11/13/burma-chronology-aung-san-suu-kyis-detention
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A rights-monitoring organization's own
  dedicated legal/chronological account of all three detention periods
  (1989-95, 2000-02, 2003-10), independent of any biography or journalistic
  narrative — supplies exact dates, the legal instruments used (State
  Protection Act), the Depayin ambush and its death toll, and the 2009
  Yettaw-trial sequence with specific charge/sentence/commutation dates.

### 2. Al Jazeera — "The trouble with Aung San Suu Kyi" (2017)
- **Type:** Major international news organization, long-form feature journalism
- **URL:** https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2017/5/12/the-trouble-with-aung-san-suu-kyi
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A dedicated critical feature, independent
  of the scholarly Journal of Democracy piece (#3) below, drawing on named
  former colleagues and a former US ambassador (Derek Mitchell) for direct,
  attributed criticism of her governing/leadership style — "she runs her
  party like a dictator," "she doesn't listen, or accept advice" — plus her
  documented silence and non-engagement with domestic media after taking
  office.

### 3. Journal of Democracy — "Burma: Suu Kyi's Missteps"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed/editorially-vetted policy and democracy-studies journal
- **URL:** https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/burma-suu-kyis-missteps/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A scholarly policy-analysis journal
  distinct in register and sourcing from the Al Jazeera feature (#2) —
  supplies the most granular documented governance detail found in this
  research: the diploma-mill cabinet-minister episode and her refusal to
  replace them, the Leninist party-discipline framing, the Mon State bridge
  naming dispute and its electoral consequence, the sequencing error linking
  the peace process to constitutional reform, and the "fake rape" Facebook
  post from her own government's information office during the Rohingya
  crisis.

### 4. Al Jazeera — "Transcript: Aung San Suu Kyi's speech at the ICJ in full" (2019)
- **Type:** Direct transcript of a primary legal/political speech
- **URL:** https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/12/12/transcript-aung-san-suu-kyis-speech-at-the-icj-in-full
- **Method:** Fetched and read (WebFetch) for the transcript's substantive
  legal/rhetorical content.
- **Why independent/substantive:** Her own words, in full, at the
  International Court of Justice defending Myanmar against the Rohingya
  genocide case — the single most load-bearing primary document for her
  later-career public conduct, distinct from any secondhand account of what
  she said. Cross-checked against CNN, Amnesty International, and UN News
  coverage of the same hearing (via search) for context and public reaction.

### 5. TIME Magazine (archive) — "Aung San Suu Kyi" profile (May 2009)
- **Type:** Major international news-magazine profile
- **URL:** https://time.com/archive/6946675/aung-san-suu-kyi-2/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A contemporaneous 2009 profile (written
  during the Yettaw-swimmer incident/trial), independent of the later
  retrospective sources above — supplies directly-quoted first-person lines
  ("I don't believe in people just hoping. We work for what we want";
  "Whatever they do to me, that's between them and me; what's important is
  what they are doing to the country") and specific documented daily-life
  detail (the 4:30 a.m. meditation routine, playing Mozart until the piano
  went out of tune in Burma's humidity).

### 6. Peter Popham — *The Lady and the Peacock: The Life of Aung San Suu Kyi* (Rider/Random House, 2011)
- **Type:** Major full-length biography, built from five years of research
  including covert Burma trips and unpublished-diary access
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a monograph, not a web
  page). Engaged through multiple independent WebSearch result sets
  (Publishers Weekly, Penguin/Random House's own listing, Goodreads, The
  Daily Beast's review, Google Books) that consistently describe its
  content, sourcing method, and specific episodes.
- **Why independent/substantive:** Popham, a British journalist, drew on
  interviews with Suu Kyi herself, her friends and family, and extracts from
  unpublished diaries — the most detailed single biographical treatment of
  her rise from 1988 through the height of her house arrest. Used here
  mainly as corroboration for the overall arc (the speed of her 1988
  political emergence, the "unwillingness to abandon her supporters and
  party" as the throughline the book itself identifies) and to confirm that
  other, thinner search-derived material sits within a credentialed
  biographical consensus rather than being an outlier.

### 7. Aung San Suu Kyi's own writings and speeches — primary sources
  (*Freedom from Fear and Other Writings* essay collection incl. the title
  essay; *Letters from Burma*; the August 26, 1988 Shwedagon Pagoda speech;
  the 1991/2012 Nobel Peace Prize acceptance lecture)
- **Type:** Primary source — her own published essays, letters, and speeches
- **Method:** NOT fetched as complete single documents (the Shwedagon-speech
  full-text page at burmalibrary.org and the Nobel-lecture pages at
  nobelprize.org and americanrhetoric.com all returned HTTP 403/timeout when
  fetched directly). Engaged instead through multiple independent WebSearch
  result sets that directly quote her own text at length — Goodreads' quote
  page for both books, a preserved PDF excerpt of the "Freedom from Fear"
  essay hosted by the Asia Society, Wikipedia's "Letters from Burma" summary
  page, the Online Burma/Myanmar Library's own description of the Shwedagon
  speech, and multiple outlets' (NPR, BBC, Inquirer) coverage of the 2012
  Nobel lecture with direct quotation.
- **Why independent/substantive:** This is genuinely primary material — her
  own stated reasoning for entering politics ("I could not as my father's
  daughter remain indifferent to all that was going on"), her own essay on
  courage and fear built around her father's example, her own retrospective
  1990 formulation ("It is not power that corrupts but fear... Fear of
  losing power corrupts those who wield it, and fear of the scourge of power
  corrupts those who are subject to it"), and her own house-arrest-era
  letters on democracy, opposition, and truth. Multiple independent
  aggregator/quote sources were cross-checked against each other and found
  to converge on the same wording, giving reasonable confidence the quotes
  are accurately transcribed even though no single full primary text could
  be fetched directly.

### 8. Multiple independent journalistic accounts — the April 1989 Danubyu
  rifle incident, the July 1989 hunger strike, the May 2003 Depayin massacre
  and escape, and the September 1988 NLD founding
- **Type:** Aggregated independent journalism/reference sources (EBSCO
  Research Starters, Irrawaddy news headlines, multiple Wikipedia-adjacent
  and biographical reference summaries, Britannica)
- **Method:** Engaged via WebSearch synthesis; the Irrawaddy full article
  itself returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, but its content is closely
  paraphrased/quoted across several independent secondary summaries that
  converge on the same specific details (e.g., "to kill and mutilate was
  their purpose").
- **Why independent/substantive:** Supplies concrete, specific episodes not
  found in the more governance-focused sources above: the documented
  incident of soldiers under a captain's orders kneeling to aim rifles at
  her in Danubyu, with Suu Kyi motioning her followers aside and walking
  straight toward the levelled guns until the order to fire was countermanded;
  the twelve-day hunger strike demanding humane treatment for 40 detained
  NLD members, ended only once the junta promised fair treatment; the
  specific mechanics of the Depayin ambush (a USDA/Swan Ah Shin militia
  roadblock, at least 70 killed, her own escape with her driver); and the
  NLD's founding leadership structure (Aung Gyi as president, Tin Oo as
  vice-president, herself as general secretary) and Aung Gyi's subsequent
  resignation over alleged communist infiltration.

### 9. Asia Times — "The unavoidable prisoner: Aung San Suu Kyi at 81" (June 2026)
- **Type:** Contemporary international-affairs journalism
- **URL:** https://asiatimes.com/2026/06/the-unavoidable-prisoner-aung-san-suu-kyi-at-81/
- **Method:** Engaged via WebSearch synthesis (direct WebFetch returned HTTP
  403); by Washington-based Burmese journalist Nyein Chan Aye.
- **Why independent/substantive:** The most current source used in this
  research, giving her documented status as of mid-2026 (moved to a

  confirmation of her condition, turning 81 in continued detention) and an
  independent, present-day assessment of how regional/global powers
  currently treat her case — used only for present-status/reception facts,
  not retrospective trait claims.
