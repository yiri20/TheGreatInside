### 1. Encyclopedia.com — "Akio Morita" (Business Leader Profiles for Students / Encyclopedia of World Biography, aggregated reference entry)
- **Type:** Reference-encyclopedia biographical entry, substantially longer
  and more detailed than a general-purpose encyclopedia article
- **URL:** https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/social-sciences-and-law/business-leaders/akio-morita
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A dedicated, business-history-oriented
  reference profile distinct in framing and detail from Wikipedia — covers
  his mother's RCA Victrola / Western classical-music influence, the
  "almost flunked out of school" ham-radio obsession, the MITI six-month
  delay in remitting the Western Electric licensing fee, his own quote that
  "government often impedes innovative change," his 1953 European trip and
  admiration for Philips's growth trajectory, detailed management-philosophy
  quotations contrasting Japanese long-termism with American
  quarterly-profit focus, his personal hobbies in later life (golf, tennis
  at 55, skiing at 60/64, scuba at 68), and the succession sequence
  (executive VP 1959 → president 1971 → chairman/CEO 1976 → board chairman
  1989) that no other source consulted here lists with this precision.

- **URL:** https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/akio-morita

- **Why independent:** Produced by EBSCO's own Research Starters editorial

### 3. CES.tech (Consumer Technology Association) — "Akio Morita, Sony and the Power of Inventive Leadership"
- **Type:** Industry-organization institutional profile (CES is the annual
  trade show Sony itself premiered the CD player at in 1981)
- **URL:** https://www.ces.tech/articles/akio-morita-sony-and-the-power-of-inventive-leadership/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** A trade-industry-association's own retrospective,
  distinct in voice and purpose from an academic or journalistic source —
  specifically frames Morita's innovation philosophy around "curiosity to
  explore" and "courage to take risks," and around designing for how
  people would actually live with a technology rather than only how it
  worked technically. Corroborates the CES 1981 Sony/Philips CD
  announcement independently of the encyclopedia sources.

### 4. Commoncog — "The Sony Walkman" (Case Library)
- **Type:** Business-case-study analysis site, built specifically to teach
  decision-making from real historical business episodes
- **URL:** https://commoncog.com/c/cases/sony-walkman/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** The single most granular, dedicated
  source consulted here for one specific episode — supplies detail no
  general biography carries: the exact February 1979 team-assembly date
  and four-month deadline, the specific engineer named (Yoshiyuki Kamon)
  who led the MDR-3L2 headphone design, the June 2, 1979 park demonstration
  with journalists on skateboards/tandem bicycles, the initial lukewarm
  3,000-unit first-month sales figure before the 30,000-unit batch sold
  out by August, and the 186-million-unit 20th-anniversary total. Directly
  corroborates and sharpens the "market research unanimously against it" /
  "I will resign if it fails" narrative found via broader search.

### 5. Time (magazine archive) — "Akio Morita & Masaru Ibuka"
- **Type:** Major news-magazine retrospective profile
- **URL:** https://time.com/archive/6679021/akio-morita-masaru-ibuka/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** A journalistic retrospective distinct from the
  encyclopedia/institutional sources above — supplies the direct quotation
  from Morita's wife at Ibuka's 1998 funeral ("In good times and bad, we
  were always together") and frames the two founders' 40+-year partnership
  and division of labor (Ibuka the engineering "dreamer," Morita the
  international-facing "pragmatist"/diplomat) as the load-bearing structural
  fact of Sony's founding era.

### 6. Akio Morita (with Edwin M. Reingold and Mitsuko Shimomura) — *Made in Japan: Akio Morita and Sony* (Dutton, 1986)
- **Type:** Primary source — Morita's own co-written memoir/autobiography
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a print monograph). Engaged
  through a direct excerpts page (richardprice.io, quoting substantial
  verbatim passages) plus multiple independent WebSearch result sets
  (summary/review sites, direct-quotation aggregations) that consistently
  quote and paraphrase the same underlying passages — the rice-cooker
  failure, the "pocketable" radio/oversized-shirt-pocket marketing episode,
  the founding "unorthodox way" management philosophy, and his statement
  that "an enemy of innovation can be your own sales organization."
- **Why independent/substantive AND why treated critically:** This is
  Morita's own first-person account and the single richest source for his
  stated motives and reasoning — but it is a curated, co-written corporate
  memoir published at the height of Sony's prestige (1986, before the
  Betamax defeat was fully digested and before the Columbia Pictures/
  "Japan That Can Say No" controversies), so it is treated here as
  self-report requiring corroboration, not as neutral fact. Every
  memoir-sourced claim used in the raw notes below is cross-checked against
  at least one independent source (Encyclopedia.com, EBSCO, Commoncog, or
  Nathan) before being treated as reasonably reliable; where the memoir is
  the sole source for a specific reasoning/motive claim, that is flagged
  inline.

### 7. John Nathan — *Sony: The Private Life* (Houghton Mifflin, 1999)
- **Type:** Independent, non-memoir biographical/business-history treatment
  of Sony and its three successive leaders (Morita, Ibuka, Ohga, Idei)
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a monograph, published the
  same year Morita died). Engaged through multiple independent WebSearch
  result sets (Publishers Weekly's own review, bookseller/library
  descriptions, and secondary citations) that consistently describe its
  content, sourcing, and argument.
- **Why independent/substantive:** Nathan, a University of California,
  Santa Barbara professor of Japanese cultural studies, was given direct
  access to Sony (commissioned in 1996 to make the company's 50th-
  anniversary film) and wrote an account explicitly built around what
  reviewers describe as "sentimental logic" — the argument that major Sony
  decisions (including, most strikingly, the disastrous ~$4.7 billion
  Columbia Pictures acquisition) were driven as much by loyalty to and
  affection for Morita personally as by hard-nosed strategic analysis.
  This is a genuinely different, more critical-of-outcomes lens than the
  encyclopedia entries or the memoir provide, even though reviewers also
  note the book's portraits remain generally admiring rather than
  adversarial — a caveat noted honestly here rather than treated as fully
  independent, skeptical scholarship. Also the source for the
  Ibuka-the-"dreamer"/Morita-the-"pragmatist" characterization and the
  Norio Ohga origin story (the critical 1953 letter about tape-recorder
  sound quality).

### 8. Springer Nature (academic publisher) — Case 16, "Masaru Ibuka, Akio Morita, Soichiro Honda, and Takeo Fujisawa: The Groundbreaking Nature of Sony and Honda Becoming Global Companies" (business-history case-study chapter)
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed/edited academic business-history case study,
  comparing Sony's and Honda's founding-generation leadership pairs
- **URL (located, paywalled):** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-19-9454-8_22
- **Method:** NOT fetched as full text (redirected to an institutional
  login wall). Engaged through WebSearch synthesis of the chapter's
  framing and the same Ibuka/Morita "dreamer"/"pragmatist" comparative
  material corroborated across bookseller and independent secondary
  descriptions.
- **Why independent/substantive:** A scholarly, comparative business-
  history treatment (Sony's founders set directly alongside Honda's
  Soichiro Honda/Takeo Fujisawa pair) rather than a single-company
  retrospective — used here mainly as corroboration that the
  engineer-visionary/business-pragmatist founding-pair structure is a
  recognized pattern in Japanese postwar corporate history, not an
  idiosyncratic framing invented by Nathan or the encyclopedia sources.
