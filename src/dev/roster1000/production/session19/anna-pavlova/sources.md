# Sources — Anna Pavlova (1881–1931), Wikidata Q151874

Research conducted via live WebSearch/WebFetch, August 2026. Eight substantive
sources used (five fetched and read in full via WebFetch; three engaged
through multiple independent, corroborating WebSearch result sets that
directly quote or closely paraphrase the underlying text, noted per-entry
below, per instruction to be honest about method). Wikipedia (both the Anna
Pavlova and Victor Dandré articles) was also used and is listed separately at
the bottom as orientation-only — it does NOT count toward the source floor.

Pavlova died in 1931; as expected for an early-20th-century performing
artist (rather than a writer or scientist who leaves a documentary paper
trail), primary sourcing is thinner than for a contemporary figure and
thinner than for a modern subject with recorded interviews/press archives at
scale. The two book-length primary biographies most relevant to her life —
Victor Dandré's own 1932 memoir *Anna Pavlova in Art and Life* (written by
her manager/companion) and Keith Money's 1982 *Anna Pavlova: Her Life and
Art* — were not accessible as full text (monograph, not a web page; the
Internet Archive copy of Money's book is borrow/stream-restricted with no
readable preview). Both are engaged only indirectly, as named, cited sources
underlying other reference works consulted here (see #2 below, and multiple
direct quotations — e.g., her last words, "get my swan costume ready" —
which numerous independent secondary sources attribute specifically to
Dandré's account). This is the same discipline the reference project's own
Louis Pasteur research session used for paywalled/monograph-only scholarly
sources (Geison, Debré): honest about not being primary-text access, used
only where corroborated across multiple independent secondary treatments.

### 1. Encyclopedia.com — "Anna Matveyevna Pavlova" (aggregating four distinct reference works)
- **Type:** Encyclopedia aggregator republishing multiple named reference
  works
- **URL:** https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/dance-biographies/anna-matveyevna-pavlova
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch), then a second targeted
  WebFetch specifically to confirm underlying source attribution.
- **Why independent:** This page reprints, in sequence, entries from the
  *UXL Encyclopedia of World Biography*, the *Encyclopedia of Modern
  Europe: Europe 1789–1914*, the *Encyclopedia of Russian History*
  (this specific section credited to Tim Scholl, a named dance historian),
  and the *World Encyclopedia* — four genuinely distinct published
  reference works, not one source reprinted four times. Supplied the
  Imperial Ballet School training detail (Cecchetti quote, "the broom"/"la
  petite sauvage" nicknames), the Firebird/Diaghilev departure, the
  independent-touring "enterprising and daring act" framing, the Mordkin
  slap anecdote, and the Uday Shankar collaboration.

### 2. Encyclopedia.com — "Pavlova, Anna (1881–1931)" (from *Women in World History: A Biographical Encyclopedia*)
- **Type:** Scholarly biographical-encyclopedia reprint, individually
  authored
- **URL:** https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/pavlova-anna-1881-1931
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch), then a second targeted
  WebFetch to confirm source attribution and bibliography.
- **Why independent:** Explicitly credited to an individual academic
  author (initialed "R. C. C.," Professor of History, Carleton University,
  Ottawa) writing for *Women in World History: A Biographical
  Encyclopedia* — a different underlying reference work from #1, with its
  own bibliography citing Margot Fonteyn's *Pavlova: Portrait of a
  Dancer*, Oleg Kerensky's *Anna Pavlova*, and Keith Money's *Anna
  Pavlova: Her Life and Art*. Supplied the most load-bearing single
  episode in this research: the 1911 Dandré embezzlement/bail crisis
  (Pavlova using her own earnings to post his 35,000-ruble bail) as a
  direct causal factor in her decision to leave the Maryinsky for
  independent touring, plus the ambiguity around the marriage claim and
  her quoted "a true female artist must be consumed in her art" line.

- **URL:** https://www.historyhit.com/culture/who-was-famed-ballerina-anna-pavlova/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).

- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** An academic reference-database product (EBSCO,

### 6. Sahapedia — "Anna Pavlova"
- **Type:** Independent India-based nonprofit arts/culture knowledge
  platform
- **URL:** https://www.sahapedia.org/anna-pavlova
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/contribution:** A specialist, geographically distinct
  source (Indian arts-history platform, not a Western general-biography
  aggregator) — supplied substantial detail unavailable elsewhere in this
  research: the 1922 India visit, the "Ajanta Frescoes" ballet (a
  documented critical failure — "amateurish"), the discovery and
  launching of Uday Shankar's dance career, her direct mentorship of Leila
  Sokhey (Madame Menaka) and her shipboard meeting with Rukmini Devi
  Arundale (for whom she arranged ballet instruction), and her attempted
  (unrealized) correspondence with Rabindranath Tagore about a ballet
  based on his poetry.

### 7. Victoria and Albert Museum (V&A) Collections — "Anna Pavlova: The Immortal Swan" poster record
- **Type:** Official museum institutional archive
- **URL:** https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1257131/anna-pavlova-the-immortal-swan-poster-unknown/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent:** A national museum's own object-record archive,
  categorically distinct from encyclopedia/journalism sources — supplied
  the otherwise-undocumented detail (via Dandré, quoted in the museum's
  own catalogue text) that Pavlova personally believed film was "the
  ideal medium for teaching the dance" and experimented with recording
  technology (slow motion in Australia, sound in Hampstead, filming in
  Java) specifically to preserve her repertoire for posterity — a
  distinct, forward-looking legacy-oriented behavior not found in any
  other source consulted.

### 8. Ausdance (Australian Dance Advocacy) — "Pavlova's 1929 Australian Tour"
- **Type:** National dance-advocacy/history institutional archive
  (Australia)
- **URL:** https://ausdance.org.au/articles/details/pavlovas-1929-australian-tour
- **Method:** NOT fetched as full text (returned HTTP 403 on direct
  WebFetch). Engaged through targeted WebSearch result synthesis that
  directly quotes and paraphrases the article's own content, cross-checked
  against a second independent WebSearch pass.
- **Why independent/substantive:** A specialist national dance-history
  organization's own detailed touring record — the most granular source
  found on the physical/operational reality of her touring life:
  itinerary specifics (Darwin bypassed on medical/logistical advice,
  Townsville/Mackay/Rockhampton/Bundaberg — regional Queensland towns with
  no prior ballet exposure), company size (~42 dancers by 1929, larger
  than her 1926 tour), and the concrete statistic that at 120 performances
  over four months, Pavlova (then 48) danced in every single one.
