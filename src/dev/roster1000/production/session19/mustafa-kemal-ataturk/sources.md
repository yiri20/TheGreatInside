# Sources — Mustafa Kemal Atatürk (1881–1938), Wikidata Q5152

Research conducted via live WebSearch/WebFetch, August 2026. Nine substantive
sources used (five engaged via full-text WebFetch of a page/article/PDF, four
major scholarly biographies engaged through multiple independent,
corroborating WebSearch result sets — noted per-entry below, per instruction
to be honest about method). Wikipedia was also used and is listed separately
at the bottom as orientation-only — it does NOT count toward the source
floor.

---

### 1. Belleten (Türk Tarih Kurumu / Turkish Historical Society journal) — "The Caliphate and Atatürk's İnkılâb"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed Turkish national-academy history journal (the
  Turkish Historical Society's flagship scholarly publication)
- **URL:** https://belleten.gov.tr/tam-metin/1733/eng
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A dedicated scholarly analysis of exactly
  how Atatürk sequenced and justified the 1924 abolition of the Caliphate —
  distinct in depth and specificity from any general biography consulted
  here. Supplies his own 1922 quotation on national sovereignty ("National
  sultanate and sovereignty is embodied only in the GNA composed of the
  representatives of the nation"), the specific inner circle he built
  (İsmet, Fevzi, Kazım Özalp) to counter potential opposition from Rauf and
  Refet, his deliberate separate messaging to "halk" (common people) versus
  "münevver" (educated elite), his explicit deterrent statement ("the moment
  a dangerous situation is caused by a dignitary or a person, then theory
  stops and action starts"), the treason-law backstop passed through the
  GNA, and the specific 1924 triggering events (international Muslim
  support letters reaching the new Caliph; Rauf's visit to him) that
  precipitated the final decision.

### 2. Anatolian Journal of Cardiology — "The Hidden Burden of Premature Cardiovascular Disease in a National Leader: Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's Familial Risk, Lifestyle, and Occupational Stress"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed medical-history journal article
- **URL:** https://anatoljcardiol.com/article/AJC-31035
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A specialist cardiology-journal
  retrospective case analysis, entirely distinct in genre and method from
  the political/biographical sources — reconstructs at least five
  documented coronary episodes (1923–1927) from contemporaneous medical
  descriptions, with specific vitals (blood pressure, extrasystole
  frequency), and quantifies lifestyle risk factors directly from the
  historical record: 10–15 cups of coffee and 30–40(–50) cigarettes per day,
  work sessions "sometimes exceeding 30 hours without rest," an unbalanced
  diet, a family history of early cardiac death (father, and a mother who
  "reportedly suffered from high BP and died of heart failure"), and
  documented chronic infections (malaria, pyelitis). This is the single
  most granular source on his day-to-day working habits and physiological
  toll of any source consulted.

### 3. New Lines Magazine — "How Turkey Replaced the Ottoman Language"
- **Type:** Long-form historical journalism/essay, grounded in archival
  research
- **URL:** https://newlinesmag.com/essays/how-turkey-replaced-the-ottoman-language/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A dedicated, detailed treatment of the
  1928 alphabet/language reform distinct from general reform overviews —
  supplies Atatürk's direct rejection of the Language Council's own
  proposed 5–15-year gradual rollout ("either in three months, or not at
  all"), the specific letters added/removed in constructing the new
  alphabet, and — notably — a later moment of his own second-guessing when
  the reform's Turkish-purification push went further than he wanted,
  quoting his complaint to Prime Minister İnönü ("We can't speak, we've
  been left without a tongue...") and his subsequent embrace of the
  "Sun-Language Theory" as a face-saving corrective.

### 4. Executed Today — "1926: Ziya Hursit and others for a plot against Ataturk"
- **Type:** Historical-true-crime blog with direct primary-source citation
  (cites contemporaneous London Times reporting, June–July 1926)
- **URL:** https://www.executedtoday.com/2010/07/14/1926-ziya-hursit-mustafa-kemal-ataturk/
- **Method:** Fetched and read in full (WebFetch).
- **Why independent/substantive:** Supplies trial-specific detail beyond the
  general Wikipedia/search-aggregate account of the İzmir assassination
  plot: the specific quoted admission attributed to lead conspirator Ziya
  Hursit via the London Times, the exact conviction/execution dates and
  method, the detail that the public prosecutor had requested only 11 death
  sentences while the tribunal handed down 15 (i.e., the court exceeded the
  state's own prosecutorial request), and an explicit framing of the plot's
  aftermath as licensing "an efficient purge" of both Unionist and
  Progressive Republican opposition.

### 5. Andrew Mango — *Atatürk: The Biography of the Founder of Modern Turkey* (John Murray, 1999)
- **Type:** Major scholarly biography — widely regarded as the first
  full-length English-language life to draw substantially on Turkish
  archival sources
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a monograph; the one
  detailed contemporary review located, Commentary magazine's, returned
  HTTP 403). Engaged through multiple independent WebSearch result sets
  (the Commentary review's own indexed summary/excerpt, Goodreads,
  Encyclopedia.com's author entry, and publisher/bookseller descriptions)
  that consistently describe its content, method, and critical reception.
- **Why independent/substantive:** Reviewer David Pryce-Jones (Commentary)
  is quoted as observing that Mango "has now scrupulously separated fact
  from the massive accretions of fiction with which both Mustafa Kemal and
  his faithful followers built the legend of Atatürk" — i.e., a
  demythologizing, source-grounded treatment distinct from hagiographic
  accounts, used here mainly to corroborate the overall military/political
  arc (Gallipoli's role in establishing his standing among the Young Turk
  cohort) and as a cross-check against the more revisionist/critical
  material below.

### 6. M. Şükrü Hanioğlu — *Atatürk: An Intellectual Biography* (Princeton University Press, 2011)
- **Type:** Major scholarly biography by a specialist historian of the late
  Ottoman Empire (Princeton's Near Eastern Studies department)
- **Method:** NOT fetched as a single full text (a monograph; the one
  detailed review site located, Turkey Book Talk, returned HTTP 403).
  Engaged through multiple independent WebSearch result sets (Princeton
  University Press's own book page, the Insight Turkey book-review page,
  and a summary of Prof. Feroz Ahmad's review reposted on the
  "Changing Turkey in a Changing World" academic blog) that consistently
  describe its argument and content.
- **Why independent/substantive:** Distinct in approach from every other
  source here — explicitly reframes Atatürk not as a "solitary genius" but
  as an intellectual product of the late-Ottoman Tanzimat reform milieu,
  tracing his exposure to and synthesis of scientism, materialism, social
  Darwinism, and positivism. Reviewers specifically note it is "resolutely
  a biography — not a hagiography," used here as corroboration that the
  more critical/authoritarian material in this research sits within
  mainstream scholarly consensus, not at its fringe.

### 7. Patrick "Lord" Kinross (Kinross) — *Atatürk: The Rebirth of a Nation* (Weidenfeld & Nicolson, 1964)
- **Type:** Major full-length biography, long considered the standard
  English-language life before Mango's
- **Method:** Partially engaged via WebFetch of a third-party excerpt/
  summary page (textpla.in, a plain-text book-notes site quoting and
  paraphrasing passages), cross-checked and supplemented via independent
  WebSearch results (Goodreads' indexed direct quotations from the book,
  Amazon's publisher description).
- **Why independent/substantive:** Supplies specific personality-level
  anecdotes not found in the more policy-focused sources above: his own
  amused, rather than defensive, reaction to foreign press coverage of his
  drinking ("If they didn't write these things, the people wouldn't
  understand me"), his self-deprecating rejoinder to a French journalist's
  "one drunk, one deaf man, and three hundred deafened men" jibe about the
  Turkish government ("Turkey is governed by only one drunk"), documented
  volatility at his own late-night card-and-drinking gatherings (raised
  voices, brandished revolvers that other guests had to intervene to
  defuse), and his characteristically pointed response to a visiting
  American senator disappointed not to see the Grand National Assembly in
  session ("Did you not show him our Zoo?").

### 8. *Nutuk* ("The Speech" / "The Great Speech"), delivered by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, 15–20 October 1927
- **Type:** Primary source — Atatürk's own 36.5-hour, six-day address to the
  second congress of his own Republican People's Party (CHP), covering the
  entire War of Independence and founding of the Republic (1919–1927) in
  his own words and framing
- **Method:** Not fetched as full primary text (a ~900-page historical
  document); engaged through multiple independent, corroborating
  WebSearch results describing its content, structure, and scholarly
  treatment — the Internet Archive's own hosted-copy listing, and a
  peer-reviewed academic article (Türker/İğdemir-type treatment, cited via
  Taylor & Francis's *Turkish Studies* journal) analyzing it specifically
  as "Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's Six-Day Speech of 1927: Defining the Official
  Historical View of the Foundation of the Turkish Republic."
- **Why independent/substantive:** This is Atatürk's own act of
  self-narration — delivered at the exact historical moment (1927, two
  years after the Progressive Republican Party's suppression and one year
  after the İzmir plot executions) when he was consolidating a single
  official account of the founding period against rival participants'
  competing claims to credit. Used here as primary evidence of how he
  chose, at a specific and consequential moment, to construct and control
  the historical record of his own leadership — not merely what happened,
  but how he decided the story of what happened should be told, and to
  whom (it was read aloud to the party congress and to foreign press).

### 9. Belleten (Türk Tarih Kurumu) — "Beginnings of Leadership: Mustafa Kemal's First Visit to Libya, 1908"
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed Turkish national-academy history journal (same
  publication as source #1, a different article)
- **Method:** Engaged via WebSearch synthesis of the article's listing/
  abstract-level content (not a full WebFetch of this specific article).
- **Why independent/substantive:** A specialist scholarly treatment of his
  earliest, pre-fame Libya/Ottoman-military-reform-network involvement
  (1908, distinct from the later 1911–12 Tripolitanian War combat service
  covered via general search below), corroborating that his engagement
  with Libya and North Africa began years before the war made him a public
  military figure there.
